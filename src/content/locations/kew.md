Kew's heritage overlays and mature established gardens make it one of the more distinctive suburbs we inspect in Melbourne's inner east. Many Kew properties have generous, established plantings that predate the pool itself, and it's common for a pool barrier here to run directly alongside garden beds, mature trees or ornamental trellis that was never designed with barrier compliance in mind. Over time, a tree planted well clear of a new fence can grow branches that overhang the barrier, or a trellis installed for a climbing rose can quietly become a climbing aid for a small child — and neither of these changes happens quickly enough for an owner to notice without a dedicated inspection.

## What a Kew pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection checks barrier height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the fence, and the non-climbable zone extending out from the barrier's outside face. In Kew specifically, overhanging branches and established garden trellis are the two things we check most closely, since they're the most common way a previously compliant barrier drifts out of compliance purely through natural growth over the years — rather than through any deliberate change to the fence or gate itself.

## Registering your pool with Boroondara City Council

Kew pools and spas are registered on the Boroondara Register of Pools and Spas, the same register that covers neighbouring Balwyn, Camberwell and Hawthorn. Older Kew properties sometimes have pools that predate current registration requirements, so if you're uncertain about your property's status, Boroondara City Council can confirm it before you book an inspection. Once certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that date so a reminder reaches you ahead of your next required inspection.

## Common reasons Kew barriers fail first inspection

The clearest pattern in Kew is vegetation-related non-compliance: overhanging tree branches within the non-climbable zone, established hedges that have grown into or over a boundary fence, and ornamental trellis or garden structures installed after the barrier was first certified. We also see heritage-style timber picket fencing with gaps between pickets that exceed the maximum allowed, particularly on older properties where the fence predates the pool. Roughly one in three Melbourne barriers fail their first inspection, and in Kew, garden growth over time is consistently the leading factor.

## Booking your inspection in Kew

The fee is $240 flat, and if issues are found, your first re-inspection once they're corrected — often as simple as a prune or trim — is included free, provided it's booked within 60 days. Further re-inspections after that are $90 each. A BPC-registered inspector carries out the visit, and a Form 23 Certificate of Barrier Compliance is issued on the day if your barrier passes. You then have 30 days to lodge it with Boroondara City Council, and we send reminders covering that deadline and your four-year renewal. Kew bookings are typically scheduled within 48 hours.
