Berwick has had one of the strongest runs of new pool installations anywhere in Melbourne's south-east over the past ten years, as newer housing estates have filled out with family homes and backyard pools to match. The City of Casey has also been one of the more active councils in Melbourne when it comes to following up pool registration, which means Berwick owners are more likely than most to have already received a letter or reminder about registering a pool or booking a barrier inspection. First-time registration and inspection requests are especially common here, often from owners who installed a pool alongside a new build and assumed the builder's fencing was automatically compliant.

## What a Berwick pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection checks fence and gate height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the barrier, and the non-climbable zone around its outer face. In Berwick, where many pools were installed as part of a newer build, we specifically check whether temporary construction fencing was ever properly replaced with a permanent compliant barrier, and whether the final landscaping — retaining walls, garden beds, paving — was completed with the barrier's non-climbable zone in mind, since builders and landscapers don't always coordinate closely on this point.

## Registering your pool with City of Casey

Every pool and spa in Berwick needs to be listed on the Casey Swimming Pool and Spa Register. Casey has been proactive in identifying unregistered pools, including through aerial imagery review, so if you haven't yet registered a newer pool, it's worth doing so before council follows up. Once registered and certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that expiry date so a renewal reminder reaches you ahead of time rather than a compliance notice.

## Common reasons Berwick barriers fail first inspection

The leading issue in Berwick is builder-installed temporary fencing that was never upgraded to a permanent compliant barrier once landscaping was finished — a mesh construction fence might have been adequate to keep the site secure during the build, but it doesn't meet the standard required for an occupied home with a pool. We also see newly landscaped gardens with retaining walls or garden edging positioned within the non-climbable zone, installed by a landscaper without specific pool compliance guidance. Around one in three Melbourne barriers fail on the first inspection, and in Berwick, incomplete post-build fencing upgrades are the most common reason.

## Booking your inspection in Berwick

The fee is $240 flat, and if your barrier doesn't pass initially, your first re-inspection once a permanent barrier is installed is included free, provided it's booked within 60 days. Further re-inspections after that are $90 each. A BPC-registered inspector completes the visit, and a Form 23 Certificate of Barrier Compliance is issued on the day for a pass. You then have 30 days to lodge the certificate with the City of Casey, and we send reminders covering that deadline and your four-year renewal. Berwick bookings are typically scheduled within 48 hours.
