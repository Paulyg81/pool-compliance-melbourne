Glen Waverley's larger family blocks have long made it one of the more pool-dense suburbs in Melbourne's east, and the suburb's popularity with renovating owners adds a specific compliance wrinkle we see again and again. A pool resurfacing, a new deck, updated landscaping or a kitchen-to-outdoor-living renovation are all common projects here — and it's very common for none of them to trigger a check of whether the existing pool fence and gate still meet the barrier standard once the work is finished. A new deck built right up to the fence line, or landscaping that changes the ground level around the barrier, can undo compliance that existed for years without anyone realising until an inspection flags it.

## What a Glen Waverley pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection assesses barrier height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the fence, and the non-climbable zone around the barrier's outside face. In Glen Waverley, we pay particular attention to how recent renovations interact with the existing barrier — a new paved area, retaining wall, or planter box installed as part of a landscaping upgrade is a common source of new non-compliance, even when the original pool fence itself hasn't been touched. We also check that any post-renovation gate adjustments haven't affected the self-closing action, which is easy to knock out of alignment during nearby construction work.

## Registering your pool with Monash City Council

All pools and spas in Glen Waverley need to appear on the Monash Swimming Pool and Spa Register. If your property has had a significant renovation since the pool was last inspected, it's worth flagging that to us and to Monash City Council, since changes to the surrounding landscaping can affect whether your existing Form 23 still accurately reflects the barrier on site. Certificates are valid for four years from issue, and we track that expiry date so your next required inspection doesn't take you by surprise.

## Common reasons Glen Waverley barriers fail first inspection

The clearest pattern in Glen Waverley is renovation-related non-compliance: new decking, paving or garden beds built up against the barrier after the original fence was certified, effectively reducing its height or creating a climbing aid where there wasn't one before. We also see storage of pool equipment, outdoor furniture or renovation materials temporarily stacked within the non-climbable zone during works and never moved back afterward. Across Melbourne, close to one in three barriers fail on the first visit, and in Glen Waverley renovation activity is consistently the leading cause.
