Cranbourne's rapid residential growth over the past decade means a large proportion of the pools we inspect here were installed alongside a new home build, often as part of a broader landscaping package finished shortly after handover. That timing creates a specific pattern we see repeatedly: a builder or developer installs temporary fencing to secure the site during construction, and once the home is complete and the family has moved in, that temporary fencing is never formally upgraded to a permanent, standard-compliant barrier. It's an easy step to miss, particularly when the rest of the landscaping looks finished and the pool is already in use.

## What a Cranbourne pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection checks barrier height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the fence, and the non-climbable zone around the barrier's outer face. In Cranbourne, we specifically look for temporary or construction-grade fencing still in place around a completed pool, and check whether any post-handover landscaping — turf, garden beds, retaining walls — was installed with the required non-climbable clearance in mind, since this is often finished by a separate contractor to the one who installed the pool itself.

## Common reasons Cranbourne barriers fail first inspection

The dominant issue in Cranbourne is exactly the pattern described above: builder-grade or temporary fencing still in place well after a home is complete and occupied. We also see newly turfed and landscaped yards where a garden bed or low retaining edge has been built directly against the barrier, encroaching on the required clearance zone. Around one in three barriers fail their first inspection across Melbourne, and in Cranbourne, incomplete transitions from temporary to permanent fencing are consistently the leading cause.
