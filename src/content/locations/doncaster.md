Doncaster has seen one of the strongest runs of knockdown-rebuild activity in Melbourne's east over the past decade, and that shows up clearly in the pools we inspect here. A large share of Doncaster pools were installed as part of a new-build project, often alongside premium landscaping and architectural fencing rather than a basic paling fence. Frameless glass panels, powder-coated aluminium fencing and multi-point locking gate systems are all common — and while these products are generally well suited to meeting the barrier standard, they still trip up first-time inspections more often than owners expect, usually because of how the self-latching mechanism was set at installation rather than any fault with the product itself.

## What a Doncaster pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection checks fence and gate height (minimum 1200mm), gaps under the barrier, the non-climbable zone around its outer face, and gate self-closing and self-latching function. In Doncaster, where frameless glass and premium aluminium fencing are common, we test gate hardware particularly carefully — magnetic latches and multi-point locking systems on glass gates need to engage reliably from any opening angle, and a gate that swings slightly off its original alignment (common after a season or two of use) can stop latching correctly even though it looks fine at a glance.

## Registering your pool with Manningham City Council

Doncaster pools and spas are registered on the Manningham Pool and Spa Register, shared with neighbouring Templestowe and Doncaster East. If your pool was installed as part of a recent build, registration is usually handled during the building process, but it's worth confirming directly with Manningham City Council that everything was finalised correctly before booking your first inspection. Once certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that date to remind you ahead of your next required inspection.

## Common reasons Doncaster barriers fail first inspection

The leading cause in Doncaster is gate hardware — specifically, self-latching mechanisms on glass and aluminium gates that don't engage reliably from every angle, or that have drifted out of alignment since installation. We also see landscaping features common to newer builds, like built-in planter boxes or feature walls, positioned within the non-climbable zone by landscapers who weren't specifically briefed on pool barrier requirements. Roughly one in three Melbourne barriers fail their first inspection, and in Doncaster, gate hardware issues on otherwise well-built modern fencing are the most frequent reason.

The fee is $240 flat, and if your barrier doesn't pass initially, your first re-inspection once the gate or landscaping issue is fixed is included free, provided it's booked within 60 days. You then have 30 days to lodge the certificate with Manningham City Council, and we handle reminders for both that deadline and your four-year renewal.
