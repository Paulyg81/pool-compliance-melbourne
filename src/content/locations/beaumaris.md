Beaumaris has one of the highest rates of in-ground pool ownership anywhere in Melbourne's south-east, and the suburb's terrain plays a bigger role in barrier compliance here than almost anywhere else we work. Many Beaumaris blocks slope toward the cliffs and the bay, which means pools are often built into split-level backyards with retaining walls, steps, and decking at multiple heights. That's a very different compliance picture to a flat block with a simple perimeter fence — every change in ground level around the pool has the potential to affect the barrier's effective height, and retaining walls that double as part of the barrier need particular scrutiny.

## What a Beaumaris pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection measures fence and gate height (minimum 1200mm from the finished ground level, whichever side of the barrier is relevant), checks for gaps under the fence, confirms the non-climbable zone is clear of obstructions, and tests that gates self-close and self-latch from any position. In Beaumaris, we spend extra time assessing retaining walls and multi-level decking that forms part of the barrier line, because these structures are more prone to settling and shifting over time than a straightforward paling fence on level ground — small amounts of movement in a retaining wall can be enough to open a gap or reduce an effective height below the compliant threshold.

## Registering your pool with Bayside City Council

Beaumaris pools and spas are registered on the Bayside Register of Swimming Pools and Spas, the same as neighbouring Brighton, Black Rock and Sandringham. If you've bought an established Beaumaris property with a pool that was there when you moved in, it's worth confirming with Bayside City Council that registration is current in your name — inherited pools are sometimes registered under a previous owner's details. Your Form 23 is valid for four years from issue, and we keep a record of that expiry date so you get a reminder well ahead of your next required inspection.

## Common reasons Beaumaris barriers fail first inspection

Sloping blocks and coastal-style landscaping are behind most of the fails we record in Beaumaris. Retaining walls or garden beds built up against the outside of the barrier can reduce the effective non-climbable zone to nothing, and established native planting — a Beaumaris favourite — can grow into a fence line over the years and create a foothold that wasn't there when the barrier was first certified. Timber decking around split-level pools is another common issue, particularly where a step or landing brings the ground level up close to gate height. Around a third of first inspections across Melbourne turn up an issue like this, and Beaumaris is no exception.
