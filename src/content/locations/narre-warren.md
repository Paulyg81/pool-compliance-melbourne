## What a Narre Warren pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection assesses the full barrier system — whether that's a conventional fence, the pool wall itself for an above-ground installation, or a combination of both — against the required height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the barrier, and the non-climbable zone around its outside face. For above-ground pools in Narre Warren, we specifically check whether external ladders or steps are removable or lockable when the pool isn't in use, since a permanently accessible ladder effectively creates a climbing point that defeats the pool wall's role as a barrier.

## Registering your pool with City of Casey

Narre Warren pools and spas — including above-ground and plunge pools — are registered on the Casey Swimming Pool and Spa Register. It's a common misconception that smaller or above-ground pools are exempt from registration; in Victoria, the requirement applies based on water depth, not pool type or permanence, so a portable pool holding more than 300mm of water still needs to be registered and barrier-compliant. Once registered and certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that date so you receive a reminder ahead of your next inspection.

## Common reasons Narre Warren barriers fail first inspection

The most common issue in Narre Warren is above-ground pools with a fixed, non-removable ladder or set of steps providing constant access over the pool wall — turning what should be a compliant barrier into an easy climb for a small child. We also see plunge pools and small spas on newer estate blocks where side-yard access gates don't self-close reliably, often because they were installed as a low-cost afterthought rather than as part of the pool's original barrier design. Roughly one in three Melbourne inspections fail first time, and in Narre Warren, above-ground pool access points are consistently the leading cause.
