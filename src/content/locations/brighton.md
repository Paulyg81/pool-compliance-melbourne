Brighton has one of the highest concentrations of backyard pools anywhere in Bayside, and a housing stock that makes barrier compliance more complicated than most owners expect. The suburb's period Edwardian and Victorian homes on generous blocks were mostly built decades before pool barrier standards existed. Where a pool has been added to one of these older properties, it's common to find the barrier improvised from whatever boundary fencing was already there — a brick wall here, a paling fence there, sometimes a hedge filling the gap. Each of these needs to be assessed on its own merits against the current Victorian barrier standard, and inconsistent fence heights along a single boundary are one of the most frequent issues we find during a Brighton inspection.

## What a Brighton pool barrier inspection covers

A standard inspection checks the full perimeter of your pool or spa barrier: fence and gate height (a minimum of 1200mm above ground level), gaps under the fence, the "non-climbable zone" around the outside of the barrier, and whether gates are self-closing and self-latching. In Brighton specifically, we pay close attention to where a pool fence meets an existing period brick or return wall, since older masonry was rarely built with pool compliance in mind and can have decorative elements — capping, pillars, low sections — that create a climbable point. Elevated pool decking, common on Brighton's near-level but often drainage-affected blocks, is another area we check carefully, since a change in ground level around the barrier can quietly drop its effective height below the compliant threshold.

## Registering your pool with Bayside City Council

Every pool and spa in Bayside must be registered on the Bayside Register of Swimming Pools and Spas, whether it was installed last year or forty years ago. If you're not sure whether your Brighton property's pool is currently registered, Bayside City Council can confirm this, and it's worth checking before you book an inspection — an unregistered pool needs to be registered first. Once registered, your barrier is required to be re-certified every four years via a fresh Form 23 Certificate of Barrier Compliance, and we track that renewal date for our Brighton clients so you're not caught out by a letter from council reminding you it has lapsed.

## Common reasons Brighton barriers fail first inspection

Across Melbourne, roughly one in three pool barriers fail their first inspection, and Brighton's numbers track closely with that average. The most common issues we see locally are: garden features (pots, outdoor furniture, low retaining walls) placed within the 900mm non-climbable zone outside the fence; gates that have been re-hung or adjusted over the years and no longer self-close and latch reliably; and side-return brick walls that predate the pool and sit just under the required height once ground level changes are accounted for. None of these are usually expensive to fix, but they do need to be corrected and re-inspected before a Form 23 can be issued.
