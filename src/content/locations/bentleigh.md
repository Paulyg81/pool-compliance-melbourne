Bentleigh's housing has changed shape more than most Melbourne suburbs over the past fifteen years, and that shift shows up directly in the pool barriers we inspect. Original post-war weatherboard homes on standard blocks sit alongside a wave of newer townhouse and unit developments, many built on subdivided former backyards. The result is a real mix of installations: older in-ground pools with timber fencing that has weathered and shifted over decades, and compact plunge pools or spas squeezed into the smaller courtyard-style outdoor spaces that come with subdivision. Each presents different compliance questions, and a one-size-fits-all approach doesn't work well here.

## What a Bentleigh pool barrier inspection covers

Every inspection assesses the barrier as a complete system — height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching hardware, gaps under the fence, and the non-climbable zone that must be kept clear on the outside of the barrier. In Bentleigh's older weatherboard properties, we frequently find timber pickets that have warped or dropped slightly out of alignment, opening gaps that exceed the maximum allowed. In the newer subdivided developments, the challenge is usually the opposite problem: barriers built correctly at installation, but boxed in so tightly against a boundary or a house wall that outdoor furniture, air conditioning units or bins end up placed within the non-climbable zone almost by necessity, simply because there's nowhere else to put them.

## Registering your pool with Glen Eira City Council

All pools and spas in Bentleigh need to be listed on the Glen Eira Pool and Spa Register, and this applies regardless of when the pool was installed or how small it is — a compact spa counts if it holds more than 300mm of water. If your property changed hands recently, it's worth double-checking registration status with Glen Eira City Council directly, since registration doesn't automatically transfer or update with a change of owner in every case. Once your barrier has a current Form 23, it's valid for four years, and we track that expiry date so a renewal reminder reaches you well before council follows up.

## Common reasons Bentleigh barriers fail first inspection

The suburb's split between older freestanding homes and newer higher-density development means the failure patterns vary by property type. On older blocks, warped or sagging timber fencing and gates that no longer self-latch are the most common issues. On newer, smaller allotments, it's almost always about clearance — pot plants, storage boxes, or a barbecue positioned too close to the fence line, effectively creating a step for a small child to climb. Around one in three inspections across Melbourne turn up a fail on the first visit, and Bentleigh's figures are consistent with that.

## Booking your inspection in Bentleigh

The fee is $240 flat, covering the inspection and your first free re-inspection, provided it's booked within 60 days of any issues being corrected. Further re-inspections after that are $90 each. A BPC-registered inspector carries out every visit, and a Form 23 Certificate of Barrier Compliance is issued on the day if your barrier passes. You then have 30 days to lodge that certificate with Glen Eira City Council, and we handle the reminders so it doesn't get missed.
