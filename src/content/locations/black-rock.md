Black Rock's coastal blocks tend to combine a pool with elevated timber decking and bushland-style landscaping that suits the cliff-top setting, and both of those things show up repeatedly in the inspections we carry out here. Established native gardens, popular throughout Black Rock for their low water use and coastal hardiness, are lovely to look at but need to be kept well clear of the pool barrier's non-climbable zone. Over the years we've found that overhanging banksia, correa and other native shrubs planted decades ago as small specimens have simply grown into the fence line, creating a climbing point that wasn't there when the barrier was last certified.

## What a Black Rock pool barrier inspection covers

A standard inspection covers barrier height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the fence, and the clearance of the non-climbable zone on the outside of the barrier. In Black Rock specifically, we look closely at freestanding pot arrangements and garden features near the fence line — a common styling choice on the suburb's elevated decks and courtyards — since a cluster of large pots stacked or positioned close together can inadvertently create a step a young child could use to climb over. Elevated decking around pools built into sloping blocks is also checked carefully, as a raised deck can quietly reduce a barrier's effective height if it wasn't accounted for at installation.

## Registering your pool with Bayside City Council

Black Rock properties are registered on the Bayside Register of Swimming Pools and Spas. If you've recently purchased in the area, it's worth confirming registration status directly with Bayside City Council, since older Black Rock pools installed well before registration became mandatory sometimes slip through if a previous owner never got around to it. Once registered and certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that date so you receive a reminder ahead of your next required inspection rather than a compliance notice from council.

## Common reasons Black Rock barriers fail first inspection

The two recurring issues in Black Rock are overgrown garden beds encroaching on the non-climbable zone, and decorative garden features — rocks, sculptures, raised garden edging — placed just outside the barrier where they create an unintended step. We also see gate hardware affected by the coastal environment more than in inland suburbs; salt air can accelerate wear on hinges and self-closing mechanisms, so gates that were compliant at installation sometimes no longer close and latch reliably a few years later. Roughly one in three Melbourne inspections fail on the first visit, and Black Rock tracks with that average.
