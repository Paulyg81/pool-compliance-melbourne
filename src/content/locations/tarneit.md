Tarneit's newer estates have brought a wave of compact plunge pools and spa installations to smaller blocks, a pattern common across Melbourne's western growth corridor. With less backyard space to work with than in older, larger-lot suburbs, pools here are often built close to side and rear boundaries, which puts side-yard access gates and shared boundary fencing squarely at the centre of most compliance questions. A barrier that shares a fence line with a neighbouring property needs the same full inspection as one that stands entirely on its own — height, gaps and non-climbable zone all still apply, regardless of who owns the fence on the other side.

## What a Tarneit pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection checks fence and gate height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the barrier, and the non-climbable zone around its outer face. In Tarneit, side-yard access gates get particular attention, since compact blocks often mean the gate providing access to the pool area is also the main side access route for bins, garden equipment or general household use — and gates used frequently for other purposes are more likely to be propped open or have their self-closing function disabled over time.

## Registering your pool with Wyndham City Council

Tarneit pools and spas — including compact plunge pools and portable spas — are registered on the Wyndham Pool and Spa Register. As in neighbouring Point Cook, registration applies based on water depth rather than pool size, so even a small plunge pool holding more than 300mm of water needs to be registered and barrier-compliant. Once registered and certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that date so a reminder reaches you well ahead of your next required inspection.

## Common reasons Tarneit barriers fail first inspection

The leading issue in Tarneit is side-yard access gates that have been propped open or had their self-closing mechanism disabled for everyday convenience, since these gates often double as general access routes rather than being used solely for the pool. We also see compact plunge pools where outdoor furniture or storage, positioned out of necessity on a smaller block, ends up within the non-climbable zone simply because there's limited alternative space. Around one in three Melbourne barriers fail their first inspection, and in Tarneit, dual-purpose access gates are consistently the most common cause.

## Booking your inspection in Tarneit

The fee is $240 flat, and if issues are found, your first re-inspection once they're corrected is included free, provided it's booked within 60 days. Further re-inspections after that are $90 each. A BPC-registered inspector completes the visit, and a Form 23 Certificate of Barrier Compliance is issued on the day for a pass. You then have 30 days to lodge the certificate with Wyndham City Council, and we send reminders for that deadline and your four-year renewal. Tarneit bookings are typically scheduled within 48 hours.
