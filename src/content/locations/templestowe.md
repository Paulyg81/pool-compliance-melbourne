Templestowe's semi-rural character sets it apart from most of Melbourne's middle-ring suburbs. Larger lots, longer driveways and pools set well back from the house are all common here, and that layout brings a specific compliance challenge: longer fence runs mean more gates, more boundary sections, and more vegetation to check along the way. A pool barrier on a Templestowe block might run past a garden shed, alongside a driveway, or through an established orchard-style garden, and each of those sections needs to independently meet the same standard as a straightforward suburban paling fence.

## What a Templestowe pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection assesses fence and gate height (minimum 1200mm), gaps under the barrier, the non-climbable zone around its perimeter, and whether every gate along the boundary self-closes and self-latches. In Templestowe, we pay close attention to secondary gates — service gates near a shed, a side gate onto a driveway, or an access point through a paddock-style rear boundary — since these are often overlooked when a barrier is upgraded, even though they need to meet exactly the same standard as the main pool gate. Long fence runs also mean more opportunity for established trees and garden beds to encroach on the non-climbable zone over time.

## Registering your pool with Manningham City Council

All pools and spas in Templestowe are registered on the Manningham Pool and Spa Register. Larger, semi-rural blocks here sometimes have pools that were installed with minimal council involvement decades ago, so if you're unsure whether your property's pool is currently registered, Manningham City Council can confirm this before you book an inspection. Once registered and certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that expiry date so a reminder reaches you ahead of your next required inspection.

## Common reasons Templestowe barriers fail first inspection

The most common issue in Templestowe is a secondary gate — one that isn't the main entry point to the pool area — that either doesn't self-close or has been propped open for convenience, particularly on properties where the pool sits well away from the house and multiple boundary sections come into play. Overgrown garden beds and established trees encroaching on the non-climbable zone along longer fence runs are the other recurring issue. Around one in three Melbourne barriers fail on the first inspection, and Templestowe's larger, more complex boundary layouts put it toward the higher end of that figure.
