Essendon's inner-north character homes sit on noticeably narrower blocks than many of the suburbs further out, and that has a direct effect on how pools are fitted in and fenced. It's common for an Essendon pool to sit tight against a side or rear boundary, sharing a fence line with a neighbouring property rather than standing on an independent boundary of its own. That arrangement is perfectly compliant when done correctly, but it does mean shared fencing and the precise position of gates relative to the non-climbable zone need particularly careful checking, since there's often very little margin for error on a narrow block.

## What an Essendon pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection checks fence and gate height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the barrier, and the non-climbable zone around its outer face. In Essendon, where pools frequently sit close to shared boundaries, we check carefully whether a shared fence meets the barrier standard on the pool side specifically — a fence can be a perfectly good boundary fence between two properties without necessarily meeting the taller, gap-free, non-climbable requirements of a pool barrier, and the two aren't automatically the same thing.

## Registering your pool with Moonee Valley City Council

Essendon pools and spas are registered on the Moonee Valley Pool and Spa Register. Given the suburb's older housing stock and narrower blocks, it's worth confirming registration status with Moonee Valley City Council if you've purchased an established property, particularly one where the pool may have been installed before current registration requirements existed. Once registered and certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that expiry so a reminder reaches you ahead of your next required inspection.

## Common reasons Essendon barriers fail first inspection

The most common issue in Essendon is a shared boundary fence that functions well as a property boundary but doesn't meet the taller, gap-free standard required specifically for a pool barrier — often because it was built to a general fencing standard rather than the pool-specific one. We also see gates positioned very close to a side boundary on narrow blocks, where the tight space makes it easy for the non-climbable zone to be compromised by whatever is stored nearby, from bins to garden tools. Around one in three Melbourne barriers fail their first inspection, and in Essendon, shared boundary fencing not meeting the pool-specific standard is a leading cause.
