Frankston's coastal and established suburban streets have one of the broadest ranges of pool ages we come across in a single municipality — everything from 1970s in-ground pools on original blocks near the beach, to recently installed above-ground pools on newer subdivided allotments further inland. That range means there's no single "typical" Frankston inspection; older barriers often need updating to meet standards that have tightened significantly since installation, while newer above-ground pools raise different questions about which parts of the pool structure count as the compliance barrier.

## What a Frankston pool barrier inspection covers

An inspection checks barrier height (minimum 1200mm), gate self-closing and self-latching function, gaps under the fence, and the non-climbable zone around the barrier's outer face — whether that barrier is a conventional fence or, for above-ground installations, the pool wall itself. In Frankston's older coastal properties, we frequently assess barriers that were compliant under a previous standard but now sit below the current height or latching requirements. In newer areas, above-ground pool access points — ladders, decking platforms, attached steps — are the more common focus.

## Registering your pool with Frankston City Council

All pools and spas in Frankston are registered on the Frankston Pool and Spa Register. Given the range of pool ages across the municipality, it's worth confirming registration status with Frankston City Council if you're at all unsure, particularly for older properties where a pool may have existed since well before registration became a formal requirement. Once registered and certified, your Form 23 is valid for four years, and we track that expiry so a reminder reaches you well ahead of your next inspection.

## Common reasons Frankston barriers fail first inspection

Across Frankston's older coastal streets, the most common issue is barrier height and gate hardware that no longer meets the current standard, sometimes combined with salt-air wear affecting hinges and self-closing mechanisms. In newer inland areas, above-ground pool ladders and decking access points that provide constant, unlockable entry over the pool wall are the leading cause of a fail. Around one in three Melbourne barriers fail their first inspection, and in Frankston the reason varies significantly depending on which part of the municipality — and which pool era — you're in.

## Booking your inspection in Frankston

The fee is $240 flat, and if issues are found, your first re-inspection once they're corrected is included free, provided it's booked within 60 days. Further re-inspections after that are $90 each. A BPC-registered inspector completes the visit, and a Form 23 Certificate of Barrier Compliance is issued on the day for a pass. You then have 30 days to lodge it with Frankston City Council, and we send reminders for that deadline and your four-year renewal. Frankston bookings are typically scheduled within 48 hours.
